Finding stray dogs in raw camera footage

Detecting, tracking and counting dogs at three community feeding spots in Chiang Mai

Phupa Denphatcharangkul · 01223049 · CMKL

Why count stray dogs?

Dog programmes need basic numbers: how many dogs, where and when.

  • Stray dogs bring risks: rabies, road accidents and poor welfare
  • Vaccination, sterilisation and feeding teams need to know where dogs are
  • Cameras at three feeding spots recorded the dogs, but nobody labelled the video
  • Goal: find and locate the dogs automatically, then turn that into visit statistics

Two hidden problems, fixed before modelling

  • Duplicates: 22 of the 447 files were identical copies. I removed them (425 clips left), otherwise visits would be counted twice
  • Wrong times: Camera1's filenames are export times. All clips looked like one afternoon, but they really span three days and nights
  • Fix: read the clock printed on the video. Standard text recognition failed on bright sky, so I trained a small digit reader on Camera3 and 4, whose filenames are correct
  • Tested on unseen clips: 99.5% of digits right, every start time within 0.13 s
4

The pipeline (runs on a normal CPU)

3 frames per second, resized to 1280×720 → YOLO11 detector, pretrained on COCO → every animal class counted as dog → ByteTrack links boxes into tracks → visits and activity

5

How I tested it

A fair test: frames picked before seeing any results, tuning and scoring kept apart.

  • 180 frames picked at random, balanced across cameras and day/night
  • I labelled them by hand: 332 dogs, 28 'hard' dogs (mostly hidden, not scored), 20 empty frames
  • Split by clip: dev half (87 frames) to tune settings, test half (93 frames) to report
  • A detection is correct if its box overlaps a labelled dog by IoU ≥ 0.5
  • Scores: AP@0.5, precision, recall, F1, with 95% confidence intervals

Why count every animal as a dog?

The detector knows dogs, but not puppies or infrared.

  • COCO, the dataset it learned from, has no puppies and no infrared images
  • 28% of the dogs it found had another top label, mostly 'cat'
  • At night it was 52%: 'sheep', 'horse' or 'cow'
  • Counting all animal classes as dog: +0.17 to +0.20 AP, night AP 0.42 → 0.76
  • No real cats or livestock appear in these scenes, so it costs almost nothing here

Tracking: fewer dogs, but real ones

0.81 → 0.95precision: share of boxes that are real dogs
0.75 → 0.54recall: share of dogs found
  • ByteTrack follows each dog through a clip. A track counts only if it lasts ≥ 2 frames and is confident at least once (settings tuned on the dev half)
  • For statistics, a fake visit is worse than a missed frame, so precision comes first
  • So the statistics are lower bounds: the dogs counted are real, but some are missed
  • Full run: 425 clips, 26,325 frames, 2.7 hours on 4 CPU cores → 1,073 dog tracks

Recognising individual dogs: the design

Detection says 'a dog is here'. Recognition says 'this is dog #12, seen yesterday'.

  • Unit = a track: several pictures of one dog, averaged
  • Free training data: same track = same dog; two dogs in one frame = different dogs
  • Model: fine-tune an embedding with metric learning (triplet or ArcFace loss)
  • Matching: appearance + time and place; a new dog if nothing is close; a person checks unsure matches
  • Evaluate on 20–30 hand-matched dogs: rank-1 accuracy and error in the dog count
